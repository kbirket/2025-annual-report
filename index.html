Senior Life Solutions • MOUD • Women’s Night

Meeting people where they are.

When Harper County’s Community Health Needs Assessment identified mental health services for older adults as a critical gap, PHC listened. Senior Life Solutions serves adults 65 and older with group therapy three times a week, individual sessions, and monthly psychiatric care — serving a 60-mile radius.

“A lot of people think this is just my life now. But that’s not true,” says Program Director Karen Maley. “We’re nice. We’re friendly. We laugh. There’s no reason not to come see us.” Referrals accepted from anyone — no doctor’s order required.

A Lifeline in the Emergency Department

Turning a crisis into a starting point.

For too long, a patient in Harper County experiencing an opioid crisis had few options: stabilize, discharge, hope they found their way to help. The nearest treatment center might be an hour away. That critical window of connection was often lost.

PHC is changing that. When a patient presents to the ED with opioid use disorder, trained providers can initiate MOUD treatment right there — no prior abstinence, no insurance approval, no outside referral required. Patients receive medication, compassionate support, and a structured connection to follow-up care within 72 hours.

The program is led by Dr. Joe Mega, with Dr. Kent Potter, DO serving as Physician Champion. Funded by a $255,000 Specialty Care Access Grant from the Patterson Family Foundation.

Assisted living • JoAnn Mast

“This is my house.”

When JoAnn Mast moved into Heritage Estates in January 2017, she wasn’t sure about it. She’d been living with her daughter Laura Livengood — had her own space, her own routines. But seizures had made it impossible for anyone to leave her alone, and the stress on the family was growing.

Nearly ten years later, at 93, she’ll tell you exactly where she stands: “I’m satisfied. It’s a good place to be.” What surprised them both was how much JoAnn kept — her independence, her choices, her voice. The staff know her by name, tease her, look out for her.

An $8,300 Harper County Community Foundation grant funded sunroom renovation — a brighter gathering space for the people who call Heritage Estates home.
